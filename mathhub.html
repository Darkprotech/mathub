⭕ Circles

A circle is a perfectly round shape where every point on the edge is the same distance from the centre. In this chapter you will learn how to calculate its circumference and area, and how radius and diameter are connected.

1. Radius and Diameter

Radius: The radius is the distance from the centre of a circle to any point on its edge.
Diameter: The diameter is a straight line that goes from one side of the circle to the other side and passes through the centre.

Imagine cutting a pizza directly through the centre. The full distance from one edge to the opposite edge is the diameter. If you only measure from the centre to the edge, you have the radius.

d = 2r

Because the diameter contains two radii, the diameter is always twice the radius. Therefore, if the radius is 6 cm, the diameter is 12 cm.

r = d ÷ 2
Example:

A circle has a diameter of 18 cm. Find its radius.

Radius = diameter ÷ 2
Radius = 18 ÷ 2
Radius = 9 cm
Common mistake: Do not confuse radius and diameter. If a question gives you diameter but the formula needs radius, divide the diameter by 2 first.

2. Circumference

The circumference is the distance all the way around the outside of a circle. Think of it like measuring the length of a piece of string wrapped around the edge of the circle.

C = 2πr
C = πd

Both formulas calculate the same thing. Use the first one when you know the radius and the second one when you know the diameter.

What is π?

π (pronounced "pi") is a special number that describes the relationship between the circumference and diameter of every circle. In Grade 7 questions, you will usually be told to use a particular approximation.

Approximation When to use it
3.14 Use it when the question tells you to use 3.14 or does not specify another value.
22/7 Use it when the question specifically tells you to use 22/7.
Example: Circumference using radius

Radius = 7 cm
Use π = 22/7

C = 2πr
C = 2 × 22/7 × 7
C = 44

Answer: 44 cm
Example: Circumference using diameter

Diameter = 10 cm
Use π = 3.14

C = πd
C = 3.14 × 10
C = 31.4

Answer: 31.4 cm
Remember: Circumference is a length, so your answer should normally have units such as cm, m or km — not square units.

3. Area of a Circle

The area of a circle tells you how much space is inside the circle. Imagine painting the entire inside of a circular table. The amount of surface you paint is its area.

A = πr²

The small 2 means that you multiply the radius by itself.

r² = r × r
Example:

Radius = 5 cm
π = 3.14

A = πr²
A = 3.14 × 5²
A = 3.14 × 25
A = 78.5

Answer: 78.5 cm²
Very important: Area uses square units. For example, cm² means square centimetres. Circumference uses normal length units such as cm.

4. Choosing the Correct Circle Formula

One of the hardest parts of circle questions is not the calculation itself. It is deciding which formula you need.

Question asks for... Formula What you need
Circumference C = 2πr Radius
Circumference C = πd Diameter
Area A = πr² Radius
Exam trick: Look at the final word in the question. If it says "area", think πr². If it says "circumference", think 2πr or πd.

5. Finding a Missing Radius or Diameter

Sometimes the question does not give you the radius directly. You can use the relationship between radius and diameter to find it.

Example:

A circle has a diameter of 24 cm. Find its radius.

r = d ÷ 2
r = 24 ÷ 2
r = 12 cm
Example:

A circle has a radius of 8 cm. Find its diameter.

d = 2r
d = 2 × 8
d = 16 cm

🧭 Geometric Constructions

Geometric construction means creating accurate shapes, lines and angles using mathematical tools such as a compass and straightedge. The important idea is that you are not simply drawing something that "looks right" — you are creating it using steps that guarantee accuracy.

🧰 Equipment You Need

  • Compass: Used to draw arcs and circles and to transfer distances.
  • Ruler / straightedge: Used to draw straight lines.
  • Pencil: Used to make accurate construction marks.
  • Protractor: Useful for checking an angle, although classical constructions can be done without one.
  • Eraser: Keep construction marks light if your teacher wants a clean final diagram.
The compass is not only for drawing circles. One of its most important jobs is to copy a distance exactly.

📐 Constructing a 90° Angle

A 90° angle is called a right angle. The two lines meet at a perfect corner. The key idea of the construction is to create equal distances on both sides of the point and then use the compass to create an intersection.

1 Draw a straight line and mark the point where you want your 90° angle. Label it A.

2 Put the compass point on A and draw an arc that crosses the line on both sides of A. Mark those points B and C.

3 Without changing the compass width, put the compass on B and draw an arc above the line.

4 Keeping the same compass width, put the compass on C and draw another arc. The two arcs will cross.

5 Use your ruler to draw a line from A through the point where the arcs cross.

The new line is perpendicular to the original line, creating 90°.

45° Angle

A 45° angle can be created by first constructing a 90° angle and then bisecting the 90° angle.

1 Construct a 90° angle.

2 Place the compass point on the vertex of the 90° angle. Draw an arc that crosses both rays.

3 Put the compass on the first intersection and draw an arc inside the angle.

4 Without changing the compass width, repeat from the second intersection. The arcs meet.

5 Draw a straight line from the vertex through the new intersection.

90° ÷ 2 = 45°.

✂️ Angle Bisector

To bisect something means to divide it into two equal parts. Therefore, an angle bisector is a line that divides an angle into two angles with exactly the same measurement.

1 Put the compass point on the vertex of the angle.

2 Draw an arc that crosses both sides of the angle.

3 Put the compass on the first intersection. Draw an arc inside the angle.

4 Without changing the compass width, repeat from the second intersection.

5 Draw a straight line from the vertex through where the two small arcs meet.

The angle has now been divided into two equal angles.
Example:

If the original angle is 80°:

80° ÷ 2 = 40°

Each smaller angle is 40°.

⊥ Perpendicular Bisector

A perpendicular bisector does two jobs at the same time:

  1. It cuts a line segment into two equal lengths.
  2. It crosses the segment at exactly 90°.
If AB is 10 cm long, the perpendicular bisector crosses AB at its midpoint, so each half is 5 cm.
1 Draw line segment AB.

2 Open your compass to more than half the length of AB.

3 Put the compass point on A and draw arcs above and below the segment.

4 Without changing the compass width, repeat from B.

5 The arcs create two intersection points.

6 Draw a straight line through those two intersection points.

That line is the perpendicular bisector. It creates two equal halves and a 90° angle.

❓ Finding the Unknown

An unknown is a value that we do not know yet. In algebra, we often use a letter such as x to represent it. Your job is to find the number that makes the equation true.

What Does "Solve" Mean?

When you solve an equation, you are finding the value of the unknown. The most important rule is that whatever operation you perform on one side, you must perform on the other side as well.

Left side = Right side

Think of an equation like a balanced scale. If you add 5 to one side but not the other, the scale becomes unbalanced. Algebra works in the same way.

1. Addition Equations

Example:

x + 7 = 15

We want x by itself. The opposite of +7 is -7.

x + 7 - 7 = 15 - 7
x = 8

Answer: x = 8
Addition and subtraction are inverse operations.

2. Subtraction Equations

Example:

x - 6 = 14

The opposite of subtracting 6 is adding 6.

x - 6 + 6 = 14 + 6
x = 20

Answer: x = 20

3. Multiplication Equations

Example:

4x = 28

4x means 4 × x.
The opposite of multiplying by 4 is dividing by 4.

4x ÷ 4 = 28 ÷ 4
x = 7

Answer: x = 7

4. Division Equations

Example:

x ÷ 5 = 9

The opposite of dividing by 5 is multiplying by 5.

x ÷ 5 × 5 = 9 × 5
x = 45

Answer: x = 45

5. Two-Step Equations

Some equations require more than one operation. Work backwards and undo the operations in the reverse order.

Example:

2x + 5 = 17

First remove the +5:
2x = 12

Then divide by 2:
x = 6

Answer: x = 6
A useful habit is to check your answer by putting it back into the original equation. If x = 6:

2(6) + 5 = 12 + 5 = 17 ✔️

6. Finding Unknowns in Geometry

The same idea works outside normal equations. Geometry questions often give you a formula and ask you to find a missing measurement.

Rectangle example:

Perimeter = 30 cm
Length = 10 cm
Width = ?

P = 2(l + w)

30 = 2(10 + w)
15 = 10 + w
w = 5

Width = 5 cm

📐 Area & Perimeter

Perimeter measures the distance around a shape. Area measures the amount of surface inside the shape. They are different measurements and use different formulas and units.

Perimeter vs Area

Perimeter: The total distance around the outside of a shape.
Area: The amount of space inside a shape.
Imagine a rectangular garden.

If you put a fence around it, you need the perimeter.
If you want to know how much grass to plant inside it, you need the area.

Rectangle

Perimeter = 2(l + w)
Area = l × w
Example:

Length = 8 cm
Width = 5 cm

Perimeter = 2(8 + 5)
Perimeter = 26 cm

Area = 8 × 5
Area = 40 cm²

Square

A square has four equal sides, so if one side has length s, all four sides have length s.

Perimeter = 4s
Area = s²
Example:

Side = 7 cm

Perimeter = 4 × 7 = 28 cm
Area = 7 × 7 = 49 cm²

Triangle

Area = ½ × base × height

The height must be the perpendicular distance from the base to the opposite vertex. It is not necessarily one of the sloping sides.

Example:

Base = 10 cm
Height = 6 cm

Area = ½ × 10 × 6
Area = 30 cm²

Units Matter!

Perimeter is a length, so it uses units such as cm, m or km. Area measures a surface, so it uses square units such as cm² or m².

Measurement Example unit
Perimeter cm
Area cm²
Writing "40 cm" instead of "40 cm²" for an area answer can lose marks.

📊 Mean, Median & Range

These three measurements help us describe a collection of numbers. They each tell us something different, so it is important not to mix them up.

Mean

The mean is often called the average. To find it, add every number together and divide by how many numbers there are.

Mean = Sum of all values ÷ Number of values
Example:

Numbers: 4, 6, 8

Step 1: Add them.
4 + 6 + 8 = 18

Step 2: Count them.
There are 3 numbers.

Step 3: Divide.
18 ÷ 3 = 6

Mean = 6

Median

The median is the middle number when the values are arranged in order. This means you should almost always sort the numbers first.

Example with an odd number of values:

8, 3, 7, 2, 5

Sort them:
2, 3, 5, 7, 8

The middle number is 5.

Median = 5

What if there are an even number of values?

If there are two middle numbers, add those two numbers and divide by 2.

Numbers: 2, 4, 6, 8

The middle numbers are 4 and 6.

Median = (4 + 6) ÷ 2
Median = 10 ÷ 2
Median = 5
Common mistake: Do not find the median before sorting the numbers.

Range

The range tells us how spread out the numbers are. It is simply the biggest value minus the smallest value.

Range = Largest value − Smallest value
Numbers: 4, 12, 9, 18

Largest = 18
Smallest = 4

Range = 18 − 4
Range = 14

Quick Comparison

Measurement How to calculate
Mean Add all values, then divide by the number of values.
Median Sort the values and find the middle.
Range Largest − smallest.
